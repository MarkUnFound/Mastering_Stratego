\section{Recommended Architectural Enhancements}

\subsection{Value Estimation}

The Q-value overestimation and scalar value collapse identified in the training analysis directly motivate two Rainbow DQN enhancements formalized in the MARQ architecture. The Dueling Network decomposes action values into state-value and advantage streams. This design suits Stratego's action space, where many positional moves share similar strategic value and only a small subset of actions (attacks, flag-adjacent moves) carry distinct utility. The C51 distributional head provides multi-modal value estimation that differentiates risky decisive actions from safe passive alternatives. Scalar Q-values provably cannot make this distinction.

\subsection{LSTM to AAREN Transition}

The LSTM prediction accuracy ceiling of approximately 18 to 22\% is the most direct evidence for the AAREN module's necessity. The LSTM-based history encoder offers principled advantages over a full DRQN architecture in replay compatibility, per-position granularity, and gradient isolation. The sequential information bottleneck inherent in any recurrent encoder, however, limits representational capacity over long game horizons. AAREN preserves all structural advantages of the separated encoder design while replacing the recurrent computation with attention-based history aggregation. This substitution addresses the compression bottleneck without modifying the replay buffer infrastructure or the DQN backbone.

The separated encoder design itself contributes to the field. Independent hidden states for each of the 100 board positions produce a spatially structured $(64, 10, 10)$ embedding tensor that preserves piece-level temporal information. A monolithic DRQN hidden state would conflate action histories across all 40 opponent pieces into a single vector, losing spatial specificity. The per-position design also maintains full compatibility with standard experience replay by storing compact embedding snapshots alongside 15-channel board tensors, avoiding the sequence burn-in cost required by full DRQN architectures.

\subsection{Exploration Recovery}

The Noisy Net sigma remained at zero throughout the entire training run. This confirms that the $\epsilon$-greedy exploration schedule exhausted its utility without the agent discovering strategies beyond the initial local optimum. Noisy Networks parameterize exploration as learnable per-weight perturbations and would provide state-dependent exploration that adapts to the agent's uncertainty rather than following a predetermined decay schedule.

\subsection{Long-Range Credit Assignment}

The sparse reward propagation analysis showed that single-step temporal-difference learning attenuates terminal reward signals by a factor of $\gamma^{200} \approx 0.134$ for mid-game decisions. Multi-step returns ($n$-step TD with $n = 5$), as specified in the MARQ framework, reduce this effective bootstrap depth and accelerate the propagation of strategic outcomes to earlier decision points. This promotes the development of long-horizon planning behavior.


\section{Computer Science Contributions}

The primary contributions of this work lie in reinforcement learning systems engineering rather than in the game of Stratego itself. Stratego serves as the experimental domain, but the following contributions are architecture-agnostic and transferable to other imperfect information environments.

\subsection{Five-Phase Curriculum Design}

The multi-phase training curriculum provides a structured methodology for transitioning reinforcement learning agents from fully observable to partially observable environments. Progressive observability reduction, combined with opponent difficulty scaling and reward annealing, addresses the cold-start problem that arises when agents must simultaneously learn basic game mechanics and hidden information reasoning. This curriculum structure applies to any domain where partial observability is introduced gradually.

\subsection{Gradient Bridge for Replay Compatibility}

The gradient bridge adds a differentiable epsilon-scaled zero-vector to stored embedding snapshots, preserving end-to-end gradient flow from the DQN loss through the LSTM encoder while maintaining the computational efficiency of pre-computed embeddings. This technique resolves a fundamental tension in replay-based training of hybrid architectures. Full sequential reconstruction at replay time produces correct gradients but incurs a 4$\times$ throughput penalty. Standard detached snapshots preserve throughput but sever the gradient path. The gradient bridge achieves both objectives simultaneously and applies to any architecture that combines a recurrent encoder with an experience replay buffer.

\subsection{Expectamax Search Integration}

The Expectamax search integration with learned belief distributions provides a principled approach to test-time decision refinement in partially observable environments. The system weights the DQN's positional Q-values against the expected combat utility computed from AAREN-derived rank probabilities. This grounds neural predictions in tactical reality and prevents overconfident attacks on hidden pieces. The hierarchical policy blending approach transfers to any domain where learned value functions must be combined with probabilistic state estimation.

\subsection{Potential-Based Reward Shaping with Phase Annealing}

The PBRS implementation guarantees optimal policy invariance while providing dense learning signals during early training. The phase-based annealing schedule multiplicatively reduces the shaping signal across curriculum phases until it reaches zero in the final league training phase. The agent transitions from shaped to purely objective-driven learning without manual intervention. This design eliminates the reward-farming failure mode observed in additive shaping schemes while accelerating early convergence.


\section{Limitations and Future Work}

Several limitations must be acknowledged despite the strong theoretical justification for the MARQ architecture's design.

The analysis establishes that the Vanilla DQN's limitations are structurally inherent, but it does not guarantee that the proposed enhancements will achieve superior empirical performance in the Stratego domain. The interaction between C51, Noisy Nets, Dueling Networks, AAREN, and $n$-step returns in a multi-agent self-play setting may introduce emergent challenges not predicted by single-component theoretical analysis.

The computational overhead of the full MARQ pipeline relative to the Vanilla DQN baseline is a practical constraint. The Vanilla DQN's rapid training iteration enabled the 37,213-episode evaluation reported here. The full Rainbow DQN configuration with AAREN may require substantially longer wall-clock training time, limiting the total number of self-play episodes achievable within a fixed compute budget.

Systematic ablation studies are required to isolate the marginal contribution of each MARQ component. The analysis predicts that each enhancement addresses a distinct failure mode, but empirical validation of this independence assumption is needed to confirm that the improvements are additive rather than redundant.

Future work should prioritize the incremental integration and evaluation of each MARQ component, beginning with the transition from LSTM to AAREN for history encoding and the reintroduction of C51 distributional value estimation. Each component should be evaluated in isolation against the Vanilla DQN baseline before the full pipeline is assembled, ensuring that theoretical predictions are validated through controlled experimentation. Introducing Stratego to a broader participant pool for human testing should be considered only if time and computational resources permit, as the primary scientific contribution resides in the reinforcement learning framework rather than the game-specific application.
